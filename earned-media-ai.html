Earned Media & AI Search

When someone asks an AI assistant about your category, the answer is assembled from sources someone else wrote. Getting into those sources is a PR problem.

The Shift What AI Cites Read Carefully What It Means More PR
01
The Shift

From Ranking to Being Cited

This page expands the AI section of PR Basics. For two decades, search visibility meant ranking on a page of links. Increasingly, the first thing a searcher sees is a written answer, from Google's AI Overviews or from an assistant like ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, or Perplexity, with a few cited sources underneath.

That changes what visibility means. Pew Research tracked the browsing of 900 U.S. adults and found that 58% ran at least one Google search in March 2025 that produced an AI summary.

Compared with about 15% of visits without one (Pew Research, 2025).
1%

Most people read the answer and stop (Pew Research, 2025).

If people increasingly read the answer and stop, the question for a brand is no longer just "do we rank?" It's "are we in the answer, and who is the AI quoting when it describes us?" The industry has started calling this generative engine optimization, or GEO.

02
What AI Cites

Mostly Sources Nobody Paid For

The most-quoted research comes from Muck Rack, a PR software company. Its May 2026 analysis of more than 25 million cited links found that 84% of AI citations came from earned sources and only 0.3% from paid or advertorial content. Journalism made up about 27% of citations in May and about 22% by October.

Independent studies agree that paid content barely appears, but they disagree about which sources lead:

Putting the studies through the PESO model sharpens the picture. Much of what AI cites is Shared media (forums, social posts, user reviews) or encyclopedic content, not news coverage. Journalism, the classic PR target, is roughly a fifth to a quarter of citations. Paid placement is close to zero everywhere.

03
Read Carefully

Why One Number Isn't Enough

Before you quote "84%"
What the studies can and can't tell you
  • They're vendor studies. Muck Rack, Profound, Ahrefs, Semrush, and Similarweb all sell AI-visibility tools. None of these reports is peer-reviewed, and each uses prompts and categories the vendor chose.
  • Definitions drive the headline. Muck Rack's "earned" includes Wikipedia, academic and government sites, social platforms, and third-party corporate content, and its own staff have had to defend that choice publicly.
  • Engines differ. ChatGPT, Google, and Perplexity favor different sources, so "what AI cites" depends on which AI.
  • It moves fast. A single change by one platform can reshuffle the top sources within weeks.
  • A citation isn't proof of influence. Models also answer from training data that's never cited, so the cited links are only part of what shapes an answer.
What holds up across every study

Paid placements almost never get cited, and AI leans on sources it treats as independent: encyclopedias, news, government and academic sites, and large communities of real users. That's the same gatekeeper logic PR has always worked under. The gatekeeper just has a new form.

04
What It Means

Implications for PR

2%
overlap between most-pitched and most-cited journalists
For a typical brand, the reporters PR teams pitch hardest are rarely the ones AI cites (Muck Rack, Dec. 2025).
~20
outlets supply most of a brand's AI citations
Same report. A short list matters more than a long one.

Treat these as vendor findings, but the direction suggests four practical shifts:

  1. Rethink the media list. Find which outlets AI actually cites for your category and earn coverage there, rather than pitching the biggest names by habit.
  2. Make the story citable. Original data, clear facts, and expert sources are what both journalists and AI systems reuse. That's the same advice as the hooks in Earning Earned.
  3. Earn your place in shared spaces, honestly. Reddit threads and reviews feed AI answers, so how real customers talk about you matters. Fake posts and planted reviews are deceptive, against platform rules, and a crisis waiting to happen (see Crises and Rumors).
  4. Get the facts right where AI looks. Keep your own site's basic facts current and easy to find. Encyclopedic sources like Wikipedia matter too, but Wikipedia's conflict-of-interest rules discourage companies from editing their own articles. The route there is coverage in independent sources that editors can cite.
You can't buy your way into an AI answer. You get there the way PR has always gotten anywhere: by giving someone independent a reason to mention you.
The takeaway
Takeaway
  • AI summaries mean fewer clicks: visibility now means being in the answer, not just ranking.
  • Every study finds paid content is almost never cited. They disagree on which unpaid sources lead.
  • By PESO standards, much of what AI cites is Shared (Reddit, reviews), not Earned. Journalism is about a fifth to a quarter.
  • The studies come from vendors with tools to sell, use their own definitions, and go stale fast.
  • The response is classic PR: target the outlets that matter, make stories citable, and earn real third-party mentions.
